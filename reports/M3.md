## Timing on F2's part at 250 MHz

The lanes accumulate into FP32 partial sums. A pipelined adder needs `A`
rotating partial sums to take one input per cycle; with `A` = 16 (D-027)
and 4 multiply-adds per lane, each partial sum must go round its loop in
4 cycles. The question was whether product + accumulator + one rounding
fits in 4 cycles of 4 ns on F2's part. Slack is the margin left in the
clock period: positive means timing is met. Harnesses:
`platforms/f2/timing/`; script: `scripts/f2/time_loops.sh`, one AWS build
instance per log.

What the logs taught:

1. **CVFPU cannot run the loop in 4 cycles at 250 MHz** (logs 1, 2). Its
   fixed stages are long, so extra registers barely help; even retimed it
   needs more than 4 cycles. It also spends DSPs and many LUTs on a
   24 × 24 multiply that BF16 inputs do not need. This led to D-038.
2. **A BF16-specific unit fits** (log 3). The exact 8 × 8-bit product sits
   outside the loop; inside it stays only product + accumulator with one
   rounding, the same bits as the spec, without DSPs.
3. **The FP32 fma's multiply must be split** (logs 4, 5). Retiming cannot
   move registers into a DSP multiply, so `fp_product` has explicit stages
   (multiply | normalize | shift below exponent 1). The vector unit's fma is
   not in a loop, so its extra latency costs no throughput.
4. **The lanes' product was at the edge until it got a register** (logs 3
   to 6). Rewrites of the same function moved the slack across zero; one
   register inside the product, outside the loop, gave a clear margin. `A`
   and the bits do not change.
5. **The review cleanups kept the margin** (log 7, on the final RTL).

Every run of every log is at the end of this report.

## Cost of subnormal support (D-016)

`Ftz` = 1 (default 0) builds `bf16_mac`'s and `fp32_fma`'s flush-to-zero
variant, bit-exact against the golden model's ftz switch: subnormal inputs
and results become zeros of their sign; the product stays exact and the
result is rounded once, at subnormal precision, before the flush. The cost
is the difference in Yosys cells. D-016 (keep subnormals, as PyTorch)
stands; the F2 resource cost comes with the first build.

## Decisions

- **D-037:** `max` is IEEE 754-2019 `maximumNumber` (the one mismatch
  found, settled for the standard; no model output changes).
- **D-038:** the lanes' multiply-add is our own BF16 unit.
- **D-039:** all FP units are our own; CVFPU and `rtl/vendor/` are removed.
- Recorded in PLAN.md and AGENTS.md: `bf16_mac` has one register inside the
  product, `fp32_fma` two; fixed-latency arithmetic pipelines have `valid`
  only, no `ready`; `Ftz` stays in the RTL to re-measure the subnormal cost.

## Obstacles

- CVFPU's timing changed the plan from wrapping a mature unit to writing
  our own (D-038, D-039).
- Logs 5 and 6 were partly rebuilt: an instance shutdown cut log 5's last
  replay (read from the live console), and log 6 came from console
  snapshots after the session's shell stopped. Both contain every result
  line.

## Open issues

- **Timing is per unit, out of context.** The lanes' loop and the fma meet
  250 MHz alone; the full design's timing comes with the first F2 build,
  and so does the F2 resource cost of subnormal support.
- **The lane adds logic around the loop.** M4's rotating partial sums put
  a multiplexer next to the accumulator; the loop's worst path is now in
  `fp_add`'s round stage, and the margin must be rechecked with the lane.
- **Mutation testing is manual.** A scripted set of mutations with
  expected outcomes would make "tests can fail" repeatable.
- **Small Yosys differences are not significant:** the same logic written
  differently moves the count (table above).
- From PLAN.md: synthesizing every module as its own top re-synthesizes
  the FMA under each parent (check leaves and the real top once lanes and
  the vector unit instantiate it); the FMA's op codes move to a package
  when a second module uses them.
