# Performance model

A first-order estimate of how fast the accelerator decodes, and what limits
it (M2). The model is `model/perf.py`. Every number below is generated by
`scripts/report_perf.py` from three input files, and a test fails if this
page and the files disagree. Change an input, then rerun the script.

## Method

For one decode token, `perf.ops()` gives the work of each command of the
command list (`model/commands.py`, which a test checks bit for bit against
the golden model's decode step). Four times follow from the work:

- **memory**: bytes read and written in HBM ÷ the HBM read rate;
- **engine**: the engine's cycles ÷ clock. The engine has `lanes` lanes of
  `macs_per_lane` multiply-adds each, and a lane computes one row at a time.
  Rows go in passes of `lanes` (a partial pass leaves lanes idle), and a row
  takes cols ÷ `macs_per_lane` cycles, but at least the `accumulators` − 1
  adds of its final sum, which run during the next row. Attention scores
  have one row per head and position (64 long for SmolLM2); p·V has one row
  per head and output dimension. Back-to-back engine commands leave no gap;
  one whose input was just written (after a norm, RoPE, softmax, attention
  or SwiGLU; D-042) adds `engine_startup_cycles`, measured at 128 lanes and
  reused by the other scenarios (an estimate there);
- **vector**: the vector unit's cycles ÷ clock, its commands (with `LOAD`
  and `KV_STORE`) in order (D-053): a group of `vector_elems_per_cycle`
  elements a cycle, and the latencies that show. A command the controller
  stops at (it reads a result not yet written, D-045) starts at the done
  before it and adds `vector_startup_cycles`; the others start behind the
  previous command's groups, so their latencies overlap. A `LOAD` adds
  `load_latency_cycles`, a `KV_STORE` `kv_store_latency_cycles` to its done
  (D-044), a reduction its tree (`reduction_tail_cycles`, and rows at
  least `reduction_row_cycles` apart). The latencies are measured on the
  RTL with SmolLM2's whole step;
- **commands**: the number of commands × `issue_cycles`, the controller's
  cost to hand one over (a guess until M7).

A token takes at least the largest time, if the four overlap perfectly, and
at most their sum. The tokens/s column gives that range (sum first). The
"in order" column overlaps memory and the engine only, with the vector unit
and the commands after them, since the controller stops at each command
until its inputs are written (D-045). The
largest term is what limits the design.

Assumptions, to revisit in `docs/architecture.md`:
- Weights and the KV cache live in HBM and are read once per token, K and
  V once per query head: the engine shares no tile between the heads of a
  group (Q-27). This costs no engine time while the memory keeps up with
  the engine (a pass reads no faster than the lanes consume), and at most
  the engine's rate ÷ the memory's rate on attention otherwise (1024 ÷ 947
  B/cycle on F2 at 250 MHz, about 8%, estimate). A p.V tile fills its
  heads' d values per position and steps its positions, so a partly filled
  last tile can wait for its fill (`perf.tile_wait_cols`: three slots,
  the first three read ahead, and a filled tile stepped 6 cycles after its
  fill, D-051).
- Activations stay on chip, and cost no HBM traffic.
- Attention's multiply-adds (q·K and p·V) run on the matrix-vector engine.
- The HBM read rate scales with the memory clock from AWS's measured rate at
  450 MHz, capped by the ports' raw width.

## Inputs

Each value has a status. "Choice" is ours to make in M2; "guess" and "to
verify" are not yet known. Our own measurements go in
`model/platforms/measured/<platform>.json`, written by the measurement
scripts: each one replaces the documented entry (value, status and source),
and the documented value stays visible next to it. The platform is cleared
when every value is measured by us, or marked "not measurable" with a
reason (D-025).
